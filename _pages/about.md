Biography
===
I am currently a physics PhD student at MIT, under the condensed matter theory group. My research focuses on solving many-body quantum systems using machine learning techniques.

I did my undergraduate studies at Nanyang Technological University (NTU) in Singapore, where I majored in physics. My undergraduate research studies wavefront shaping, non-Hermitian physics, and computational spectroscopy.

Research Interests
===
**Machine learning for quantum many-body systems**

Many-body quantum systems are notoriously difficult to solve becuase of the exponential growth of the Hilbert space. Recently, machine learning techniques have been proven to be powerful tools for representing and solving many-body quantum states. I am currently working on solving low dimensional bosonic systems using self-attention transformer architectures.

**Computational Spectroscopy**

Unlike conventional spectroscopy, computational spectroscopy combines machine learning techniques with experimental data to extract frequency information from complex signals. The computational spectrometers can achieve higher resolution and bandwidth with much smaller physical size. Particularly, we realize that *Fisher information* plays a crucial role in the design of computational spectrometers. One can optimize (miniataturize) the spectrometer design by maximizing the Fisher information.

**Non-Hermitian physics**

Non-Hermitian physics is a rapidly growing field that studies systems with non-Hermitian Hamiltonians. i.e., systems with energy gain and loss. A striking phenomenon in non-Hermitian physics is the non-Hermitian skin effect, where an extensive number of eigenstates are localized at the boundary of the system, which violates the conventional bulk-boundary correspondence. In my previous work, I studied how non-Hermitian behavior can emerge in a Hermitian system with strong interactions, and how the non-Hermitian skin effect can be observed in such a system.
