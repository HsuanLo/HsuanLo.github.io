<h2>Research Interests</h2>
<hr />

<div class="list__item">
  <article class="archive__item">
    <h3 class="archive__item-title home__interest-title">Machine Learning for Quantum Many-Body Systems</h3>
    <p class="home__text">Many-body quantum systems are notoriously difficult to solve because of the exponential growth of the Hilbert space. Recently, machine learning techniques have proven to be powerful tools for representing and solving many-body quantum states. I am currently working on solving low-dimensional bosonic systems using self-attention transformer architectures.</p>
  </article>
</div>

<div class="list__item">
  <article class="archive__item">
    <h3 class="archive__item-title home__interest-title">Computational Spectroscopy</h3>
    <p class="home__text">Computational spectroscopy combines machine learning techniques with experimental data to extract frequency information from complex signals. Computational spectrometers can achieve higher resolution and bandwidth with much smaller physical size. In particular, Fisher information plays a crucial role in the design of computational spectrometers, allowing the spectrometer design to be optimized and miniaturized by maximizing the Fisher information.</p>
  </article>
</div>

<div class="list__item">
  <article class="archive__item">
    <h3 class="archive__item-title home__interest-title">Non-Hermitian Physics</h3>
    <p class="home__text">Non-Hermitian physics studies systems with non-Hermitian Hamiltonians, including systems with energy gain and loss. A striking phenomenon in this field is the non-Hermitian skin effect, where an extensive number of eigenstates localize at the boundary of a system and violate conventional bulk-boundary correspondence. In my previous work, I studied how non-Hermitian behavior can emerge in a Hermitian system with strong interactions, and how the non-Hermitian skin effect can be observed in such a system.</p>
  </article>
</div>
